Best Grow Bags for Vegetables: 7 Fabric Pots Tested and Compared

By The Balcony Harvest Team Published September 21, 2026 11 min read

Heads up: this article contains affiliate links. If you buy through them, we may earn a small commission at no extra cost to you. Read our full disclosure.

Fabric grow bags are the default container of balcony gardening for good reason: they're cheap, light to carry upstairs, and they air-prune roots so plants don't strangle themselves. But not all grow bags are equal. We grew in 7 popular brands for one full season — tomatoes, peppers, and herbs — and measured what actually matters.

How We Tested (Methodology and What We Measured)

We bought every bag at retail — no free samples, no sponsored placements — in 3-gallon and 5-gallon sizes, and ran them on a south-facing balcony from May through September. Each bag held the same potting mix recipe and the same watering schedule. We scored four things:

  • Drainage and dry-down: how fast excess water exits after a heavy watering, and whether the bottom stayed soggy. We lifted bags and checked saucers 30 minutes after watering.
  • Handle strength when wet: a wet 5-gallon bag of soil weighs 40–50 lbs. We lifted each bag by its handles weekly. Handles that stretched, tore, or pulled stitching lost points fast — this is the #1 real-world failure.
  • UV and weather durability: full-sun exposure all season. We looked for fading, thinning fabric, and frayed seams in September.
  • Root air-pruning: at season's end we dumped each bag and checked whether roots had air-pruned (fine, fibrous tips) or circled the walls like they do in plastic pots.

We deliberately didn't score "looks" beyond a note — on a balcony, function beats aesthetics, and most fabric bags look fine from six feet away.

Best Overall: Vivosun 5-Pack

The Vivosun bags hit the sweet spot of every metric. The 300g fabric drained well without drying out too fast, the double-stitched handles survived a season of wet 45-lb lifts without stretching, and the seams showed no fraying by September. Roots air-pruned cleanly — tomato root balls came out as dense, fibrous mats with zero circling. At roughly $3 per bag in the 5-pack, the value is hard to argue with.

Best Budget: Gardzen 6-Pack

Nearly identical performance to our top pick at an even lower per-bag price: same 300g fabric, same drainage, handles that held up all season. The only difference: slightly less structured sides, so bags slump a bit when half-filled. Fill them fully and you'll never notice.

Best for Large Plants: AC Infinity

Thicker, more structured fabric with genuinely heavy-duty handles — the only bag we'd confidently lift fully wet at the 10-gallon size. It costs more per bag, but for a tomato or pepper plant you'll keep for 5 months, the sturdiness pays off. The structured walls also hold their shape on windy balconies.

Best Small Sizes: Smart Pot

Smart Pot invented this category, and the original geotextile fabric still air-prunes as well as anything. It shines in the 1–3 gallon range: the breathable fabric keeps small herb pots from staying soggy, the usual killer of potted basil and mint.

Fabric Pots vs. Plastic vs. Ceramic on a Balcony

Quick honest comparison for renters:

  • Fabric: lightest to carry, best drainage, roots air-prune, fold flat for winter storage, won't crack in a freeze. Downside: they dry out faster than plastic — plan on more frequent watering in heat. Best default for vegetables.
  • Plastic: cheapest, retains moisture longest (good for forgetful waterers), but roots circle the walls and pots crack in freezes. Fine for herbs if you already own them.
  • Ceramic/terracotta: prettiest, heaviest (a wet 5-gallon terracotta pot can top 60 lbs — check your lease), and terracotta wicks moisture so fast it's the thirstiest option of all. Use as decorative cachepots around a fabric bag, not as the primary planter for vegetables.

FAQ

Do grow bags dry out faster than plastic pots?
Yes — the breathable fabric evaporates moisture from all sides, so expect to water roughly 20–30% more often in summer heat. Mulching the surface and grouping pots together cuts the difference. The tradeoff is worth it: that same breathability is what prevents root rot and soggy bottoms.
How long do fabric grow bags last?
Expect 3–5 seasons from a quality 300g bag with basic care. UV is the main enemy — storing them dry indoors over winter roughly doubles their life. The ultra-cheap thin bags often don't survive a second season.
Can you reuse grow bags?
Yes. At season's end, dump the soil, shake the bag out, and let it dry fully. A quick scrub with a stiff brush removes salt crust. Don't machine-wash them — it breaks down the nonwoven fibers. Refresh the soil mix each spring rather than reusing it straight.
Do I need saucers under grow bags?
On a balcony, absolutely — water drains freely from every side, and your downstairs neighbor will notice. Use rigid saucers slightly wider than the bag; flexible ones buckle under 40+ lbs.
